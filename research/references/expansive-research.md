# Expansive Research

This reference is not a separate mode. Use it only as a graph-expansion
discipline inside the same maximum-saturation protocol and the same single
Markdown research record.

## Research Graph

When this reference is loaded, treat research as a graph of useful nodes, not a
fixed list of first-pass queries. Nodes can be sources, institutions, authors,
laws, standards, datasets, methods, claims, counterclaims, corrections, or
terms.

Every material node becomes a frontier queue item in `## Lead Ledger` or
`## Expansion Frontier Audit`. It must be followed, closed as duplicate or
unable to change the answer, blocked with confidence effect, or converted into
coverage debt before firm synthesis.

For each strong node, extract query leads:

| Lead Type | Follow When |
|---|---|
| `cited-source` | A source names a report, paper, law, filing, or document not yet checked. |
| `dataset` | A claim depends on data, statistics, survey results, or measurements. |
| `methodology` | The source's method affects interpretation or comparability. |
| `institution` | An agency, lab, company, regulator, court, or standards body is authoritative. |
| `author` | A researcher, reporter, official, or expert appears central to the evidence trail. |
| `law-or-standard` | A legal, regulatory, or technical claim depends on a governing text. |
| `counterclaim` | A credible source disputes or qualifies a central claim. |
| `update` | The topic may have newer releases, revisions, amendments, or follow-up statements. |
| `correction` | The source may have an erratum, retraction, correction, or official denial. |
| `related-term` | A newly discovered term is likely to unlock better sources without drifting off topic. |
| `blocked-source` | A primary, original, full-text, official, or source-of-truth path is inaccessible and needs authorized alternate retrieval. |

## Lead Priority

Follow leads that are likely to improve the investigation:

- directly clarify a central claim, subquestion, or decision point
- point to primary, official, original, independent, or corrective evidence
- resolve an unresolved gap or conflict
- add a missing source family, language, jurisdiction, method, or date range
- improve provenance for a repeated claim
- recover a blocked primary, original, governing, full-text, dataset, archive,
  repository, docket, or method source through authorized alternate paths

Do not follow leads that are merely adjacent, promotional, repetitive, or likely to produce weaker summaries of already collected evidence.

## Exhaustion Point

Stop expanding only after frontier queue convergence is recorded in the single
research record. Strong leads must be followed and additional searches must show
sharply diminishing returns:

- new results repeat the same source lineage, press release, wire story, paper, dataset, or official document
- new query leads become tangential to the research goal
- source quality drops below already collected candidates
- no new primary, official, independent, or corrective sources appear
- remaining gaps are unlikely to be solved by more search
- blocked source-retrieval leads have been recovered through authorized
  alternates, closed, blocked with confidence effect, or downgraded

Continue when claims conflict, primary sources are missing, provenance is
unclear, source-family coverage is thin, high-value lead types remain
unexamined, or freshness remains unresolved.

## Follow-Up Passes

Use follow-up research passes whenever first-pass results produce high-value
query leads. Keep each follow-up narrow:

- focus on one lead cluster, disputed claim, or missing source family at a time
- look for strong sources, query leads, and conflicts or gaps
- stop when returned leads no longer improve relevance, authority, independence,
  freshness, frontier queue convergence, or blocked-source recovery
