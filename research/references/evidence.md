# Evidence Decisions

## Select Material Evidence

Consider each candidate family, but activate it only when its strongest reachable
evidence could change an important claim, confidence, or decision.

| Need | Candidate evidence | Material checks |
|---|---|---|
| Official truth | governing records, laws, standards, filings, product docs | authority, scope, effective version |
| Current status | changelogs, dockets, advisories, corrections, latest updates | date, supersession, retraction |
| Empirical result | datasets, surveys, papers, methods, appendices | sample, denominator, unit, uncertainty |
| Counterevidence | criticism, failures, negative findings, disputes | directness, strength, relevance |
| Provenance | original source, archive, author, funding, affiliations | lineage, incentives, independence |
| Comparison | official specifications, prices, benchmarks, procurement, user evidence | common criteria, missing data, tradeoffs |
| Technical behavior | source code, commits, releases, issues, registries | version, reproducibility, maintainer status |

Omitting a material family is incomplete research. Opening an immaterial family
is wasted work, not extra rigor.

## Verify By Claim Type

- **Current:** check latest status, effective dates, version history,
  supersession, corrections, and recent conflicts.
- **Quantitative:** check numerator, denominator, unit, method, sample, period,
  geography, uncertainty, and comparability.
- **Causal or evaluative:** separate association, mechanism, direct causal
  evidence, and inference.
- **Comparative or advisory:** define criteria and alternatives, normalize like
  evidence, expose missing data and tradeoffs, and test material assumptions.
- **Absence:** record searched terms, source families, languages, dates,
  jurisdictions, and access limits; missing evidence is not a factual no.
- **High-stakes:** do not make a firm medical, legal, financial, security,
  safety, investigative, or reputational claim from one weak or derivative
  source; require reachable authority and independent corroboration or lower
  confidence.
- **Translated or provenance-sensitive:** preserve the original locator, check
  ambiguous terms in context, and examine authorship, funding, source lineage,
  incentives, independence, and selective quotation.
